# Source and Evidence Standard

## Source priority

Prefer sources in this order:

1. contemporaneous primary sources;
2. critical editions or academic research;
3. museums, archives, institutes, and universities;
4. sourced syntheses;
5. popular sources only for discovery, weak corroboration, or a trail to stronger sources.

## Claim matching questions

Ask all three questions before assigning a high evidence level:

1. Is the source from the same period and polity as the claim?
2. Is it about the same institution, object, practice, role, or procedure?
3. Does it support the exact specificity of the claim, not merely a broader or adjacent point?

## Temporal transfer and LATER_ANALOGY

Later material may help artists and writers understand a family resemblance, survival of a practice, or research direction, but it must be labeled `LATER_ANALOGY` in notes or domain language and must not be transferred backward as proof for an earlier date. If a later analogy is the only support for a material detail, the production decision should usually be `HOLD` or `REMOVE`, and the allowed use should be limited to `visual analogy only` or `production note only`.

## Citation minimum

A source-backed claim needs enough information for another reviewer to find the same passage:

- author, editor, institution, or responsible body;
- document or article title;
- year, edition, version, or access context when available;
- locator such as page, folio, chapter, section, line, timestamp, archive ID, or table;
- URL, DOI, archive identifier, shelfmark, or other durable identifier when available.

## Conflicting sources

When sources conflict:

1. record the conflict instead of hiding it;
2. prefer stronger source classes and closer temporal/institutional matches;
3. reduce specificity when no source can decide the conflict;
4. mark the claim `UNRESOLVED` when production cannot safely proceed;
5. do not let a convenient source override a better but less convenient source.

## Design sources are not historical evidence

Game design methods, reference games, market analysis, UX standards, and production constraints can justify mechanics or presentation choices. They cannot serve as historical evidence for Đại Việt, offices, law, chronology, ritual, material culture, documents, or vocabulary.
